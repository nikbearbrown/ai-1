I have watched a particular thing happen many times now, in classrooms and in faculty offices and on calls with people who know their field cold. Someone with twenty years of real expertise sits down with an AI model, asks it to write something, and gets back four paragraphs that read beautifully and say nothing they would have said. They can feel that it is wrong. They often cannot say why. And too often they ship it anyway, because the prose is fluent and fluency is persuasive.

That gap — between what the model can produce and what a trained human knows — is the subject of this book. I have come to believe that AI fluency is not tool use. It is judgment: knowing what to ask for, what to keep, what to throw away, and what only you can supply. A powerful model in the hands of someone without that judgment is a microscope sitting in a box. Expensive, impressive, and useless until a trained person knows what to do with it.

This book is a working demonstration of the alternative. It walks you through building one real artifact — a publishable textbook in your own field — with one human and AI working together. That is what the title means: not AI instead of you, and not you laboring without AI, but AI plus one. The plus-one is the part that matters. You are the plus-one.

I wrote it now because the bottleneck has moved. Making is no longer the hard part; a model will draft a chapter in minutes. The hard part is everything around the making — deciding what is worth saying, checking that it is true, catching the sentence that sounds right and is wrong. This book is about that work, because that work is what remains irreducibly yours.

It does not promise that AI will write your book for you. It promises the opposite: that the book will be yours, and better, because you did the parts the machine cannot.
